# Hackathon rules (summary)

Summarized from the Official Rules and the Participant Guide & Packet for the TrialWatch × AI Hackathon. If anything here differs from the official documents, the official documents win.

## The event
- **FairTrial & AI Hackathon**, organized by TrialWatch at the Human Rights Institute, Columbia Law School.
- On campus: **Jerome Greene Annex, 435 W 116th St, New York, NY**.
- A competition of skill. No purchase or payment is needed to enter or win.

- Jerome Greene Annex: Fri 10 AM–5 PM, Sat 10 AM–3:30 PM. Meals, snacks and drinks are provided.

## Eligibility and teams
- Current Columbia University students (any school or program, full- or part-time, graduate or undergraduate) or Columbia alumni.
- At least 18 years old.
- Teams of **2 to 4**, with one **Team Lead** who handles all submissions and communications.

## Registration (by Thu 8 Oct, 11:59 PM ET)
The Team Lead submits, through the registration link: the track (1–3) and the names and roles of all members.

## Tracks
Each team picks one track and is judged within it. We chose **Track 2: Observation to Legal Evaluation** (see [02_track2_brief.md](02_track2_brief.md)).

## Mentoring
Three sessions with a TrialWatch expert: Fri 1 PM, Fri 4 PM and Sat 11 AM. They focus on the usability and feasibility of ideas and tools.

## Presentation and demo
- Final deliverables are due by **3:30 PM Saturday**. Then each team gives a **10–15 minute demo** to a panel of judges at the Annex, with a fixed time that includes questions.
- Bring a working prototype, a demo, or a clearly documented proof of concept. Any format works: slides, live demo, mockups.

## Submission checklist (before presenting)
- [ ] Link to a GitHub repository (or equivalent) with the code, documentation, and a README with setup and usage instructions
- [ ] An open-source license (see choosealicense.com)
- [ ] A short written description of the tool and its intended use by TrialWatch
- [ ] All code open source (closed-source code is not permitted)

## Judging (100 points)
| Criterion | What judges ask | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Innovation | Is the solution rethinking what is possible in trial monitoring and protection of those at risk? | 25 |
| Feasibility | Can the tool be realistically implemented within TrialWatch's existing infrastructure? | 25 |
| **Human Rights Impact** | Does the tool advance the right to a fair trial or freedom of expression, or support TrialWatch's advocacy mission? | **30** |
| Ethical Rigor & Responsible AI | How thoroughly does the team address potential harms, bias, and responsible AI concerns? | 20 |

## Awards
Three teams are chosen by overall points: TrialWatch Fair Trial Grand Award (1st), Best Technical Innovation Award (2nd) and Best Human Rights Impact Award (3rd). The rules list a $170 Visa gift card per team for each.

## Intellectual property and data
- Teams keep ownership of their original work. HRI and TrialWatch get a non-exclusive license to reference, display and describe the project for non-commercial advocacy and education, with attribution.
- All submitted code must use an open-source license.
- Any pre-existing third-party material must be properly licensed and disclosed.
- **No confidential, proprietary or sensitive personal data** in submissions.
- From the packet: some data sources are browse-only. If you scrape, check the site's terms and rate-limit your requests. Disclose and attribute every source. (The packet points to "Section 11" for this; in the Official Rules, the licensing language is in Section 13.)
- The TrialWatch India Defamation Dataset is available on request and probably needs a signed NDA: mjl2241@columbia.edu.

## Conduct
The organizers can disqualify a team that:
- breaks the rules;
- behaves dishonestly, disruptively or in a harassing way;
- materially misrepresents its submission;
- includes harmful, offensive or inappropriate content.

Columbia policies and applicable laws apply. Disqualification decisions are final.

## Privacy
By registering, participants agree that HRI may use their names, project descriptions, photos and submitted materials for promotion and education. Contact details are used only to run the hackathon.

## Changes
The organizing committee can modify, postpone or cancel the event, and will tell participants about significant changes.
